Why this exists

The honest version should be the fast one

Right now it isn't. A screenshot takes four taps: capture, crop, caption, post. The source is usually gone by the second one.

Genius spent a decade and $77.7 million trying to fix this for song lyrics and text alone, and still lost the war on distribution — the annotations only ever lived on Genius. It sold for roughly what it raised.

Riff isn't trying to be the platform. It's trying to be the fastest way to do the honest thing, in the browser you already have open.

Three steps, no account

From "I have a take" to "it's saved" in one motion

01

Highlight or right-click

Select a passage on any page, or right-click a video or podcast player to grab up to 90 seconds at the current timestamp.

Shortcut: Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+K
02

Add your take

Type it, or record a voice note, right there in the sidebar. Tag it if you want to find it again later.

Live autocomplete pulls from tags you've already used
03

Keep it, or hand it off

Every riff lives in your local, searchable archive. Export it as a real page whenever you want to share it.

The exported page links back — it never re-hosts the file
What's actually in it

Built for people who comment on things for a living, or just can't help it

Text, video, or audio

One capture flow for a quoted passage, a YouTube moment with the real title and channel, or a podcast beat — up to 90 seconds each.

Tags that actually help

Click any tag to filter. Rename or delete one across your whole archive from Settings. Autocomplete suggests as you type.

Archive, don't delete

Soft-hide a riff without losing it. Everything destructive comes with an undo — single or bulk.

Related clips

Open any riff and see what else you've saved from the same source, without hunting for it.

Keyboard-first

/ to search, N for a new riff, arrow keys to move through the feed.

Real exports

A finished, self-contained HTML page per clip — not a screenshot of your archive, an actual shareable artifact.

Copyright posture

It never rips the file

A YouTube clip in Riff plays through YouTube's own embedded player, cued to your timestamp. Nothing gets downloaded, re-hosted, or stripped of where it came from.

If a clip is yours and you want it gone, the source link is one tap away on every card — no takedown form required to find out who to ask.

Open and auditable

No server, no account, no dependencies

Riff runs entirely in your browser. No framework, no bundler, no npm install. Every file loads exactly as it's written, which means the whole codebase is auditable in one sitting.
